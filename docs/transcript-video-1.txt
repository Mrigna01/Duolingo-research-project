For Project 1, my target company is Duolingo, and the decision is whether a buy-side investment committee with no existing position should initiate, watch or defer, or not initiate the stock.

In Edition A, my preliminary recommendation was Watch or Defer. At that point, I had identified Duolingo as a strong-growing business, but I was still concerned about whether its profitability and monetization could justify the valuation. I had also identified important unknowns around sustainable growth, margins, and what assumptions would actually be required to support the market price.

Most importantly, in Edition A, I did not yet have a complete valuation model, so Watch or Defer was still a preliminary research judgment.

In Edition B, the recommendation remains Watch or Defer, but now I can support that judgment quantitatively. My final five-year pro forma DCF gives a base value of $137.57 per share, with causal low, base, and high cases of $114.20, $137.57, and $165.01.

That compares with Duolingo's September 21 market price of $149.42.

The biggest change from Edition A to Edition B was the valuation architecture underneath the recommendation. My early Lab 6 valuation was about $252 per share because it started with Duolingo's reported company free cash flow and projected that cash flow directly.

As the project developed, I replaced that approach with a full five-year three-statement operating forecast that drives FCFF. I also incorporated filed H1 2026 information, partial-year timing, a dated liquidity bridge, and terminal reinvestment.

So the change in valuation was not caused by one assumption. It came from several improvements in how the business and its cash flows were modeled.

One AI-assisted output I independently checked was the use of Duolingo's reported company free cash flow as FCFF in the DCF.

The reported free cash flow number itself, about $360 million, was arithmetically correct based on the 2025 filing. But I qualified the AI interpretation because company free cash flow is not the same as operating FCFF for this valuation.

Operating cash flow includes stock-compensation add-backs and interest income, while my final model treats stock compensation as an operating shareholder cost and values excess liquidity separately.

So I modified the model to calculate FCFF from operating NOPAT plus depreciation, less capital expenditure and operating working-capital investment.

So while the recommendation stayed Watch or Defer from Edition A to Edition B, the evidence supporting that decision became substantially stronger and more testable.
